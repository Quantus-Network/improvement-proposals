## Abstract

This document describes a security protocol built on top of reversible transactions in which reversal periods are mandatory 
and a separate account is assigned the role of "guardian" and has the exclusive privilege to reverse transactions and recover funds.

## Motivation

Security and convenience are naturally in tension with one another. The simple rules of blockchain transfers enable both
legitimate and fraudulent users to easily send money without fear of reversal. Anyone who steals a private key or gains 
control of a signer can send funds and the legitimate owner is generally powerless to stop it. 

A high-security account puts a mandatory reversal period on all transfers that take money from the account. This gives the 
guardian time to react. The guardian is a separate account (preferably higher-security, hardware wallet, multisig, etc.)
that has the exclusive right to cancel the transaction and take custody of the funds. 

This structure mimics the traditional financial system which can force transfer from a bank account given a court order.
The difference is that users can opt in to this system and choose their own guardian, which could be a wallet they control, 
some trusted third party, or even another high-security account. This makes the protocol composable, enabling arbitrary 
hierarchies of keys with the guardian having superior permissions to the high-security account.

## Specification

This protocol builds on top of [reversible transfers (QIP-0010)](qip-0010.md). Users can opt in via
[`set_high_security`](https://github.com/Quantus-Network/chain/blob/main/pallets/reversible-transfers/src/lib.rs),
which is permanent (re-enrollment is rejected with `AccountAlreadyHighSecurity`; there is no guardian-update extrinsic)
and requires them to specify the mandatory reversal delay and a guardian account.
The guardian cannot be the account itself. The guardian can be any kind of account, including another high-security account and/or a multisig.

While high-security is enabled:

- Outgoing transfers must use the configured delay via high-security-only [`schedule_transfer`](https://github.com/Quantus-Network/chain/blob/main/pallets/reversible-transfers/src/lib.rs); caller-chosen delays (`schedule_transfer_with_delay`) are rejected.
- For per-transfer [`cancel`](https://github.com/Quantus-Network/chain/blob/main/pallets/reversible-transfers/src/lib.rs): cancel authority and fee policy are frozen at schedule time in `pending.guardian` / `pending.from`. Only that guardian may cancel; cancelled funds are sent to the guardian with the high-security volume fee applied (`VolumeFee` / runtime `HighSecurityVolumeFee`, currently 1%), not back to the high-security account.
- [`recover_funds`](https://github.com/Quantus-Network/chain/blob/main/pallets/reversible-transfers/src/lib.rs) is intentionally asymmetric: it authorizes against the **live** guardian in `HighSecurityAccounts`, seizes every pending hold with the volume fee applied, and sweeps remaining free balance to that guardian. High-security status and the guardian relationship are preserved after recovery.
- A single guardian may protect only up to `MaxGuardianAccounts` accounts (runtime value 32). `GuardianIndex` is the storage map that tracks the protected-account list per guardian.

## Reference Implementation

High security accounts are implemented in the reversible-transfers pallet
[here](https://github.com/Quantus-Network/chain/tree/main/pallets/reversible-transfers).

## Rationale

Setting an account as high-security cannot be turned off to prevent a thief from turning it off. The guardian is expected
to be another account from a different seed phrase, preferably better secured than the high-security account. Cancelled
transfers are sent to the guardian to prevent an endless tug-of-war in the case of theft of the high-security account's 
private key.

Freezing cancel authority at schedule time in `pending.guardian` is defensive: it stops a later `set_high_security`
from retroactively rewriting cancel rights and burning a volume fee on transfers that were scheduled while the account
was still ordinary. Re-enrollment is already blocked today, but the freeze keeps one-time / pre-enrollment schedules
safe under that storage model. `recover_funds` deliberately uses the live guardian so a configured guardian can seize
all pending holds in one call.
